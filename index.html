A weird experiment I've been trying the last few weeks is having Claude take over day-to-day maintenance of our apps. Seeing early signs of life that this might be possible.

The setup is straightforward: we have a Slack channel called proj-claude-maintains-apps. In it, Claude Tag runs a bunch of daily routines across iOS, Android, Desktop, web, CLI, and Agent SDK:
- Crash fuzzer: open the app in a simulator and tap around to find ways to crash it, then root cause and fix the crashes
- Dup unifier: scans the codebase for similar-yet-slightly-divergent abstractions, and puts up PRs to unify them
- Dead-code remover: removes statically unreachable code, and adds logging to suspected dead code to check if it's really dead and if so, remove it the next day
- Abstraction police: fixes leaky abstractions
- a bunch more..

Results have been surprisingly positive. Over the last few weeks, these routines have opened 388 PRs across our repos, 180 of which we merged after Claude Code Review + human review. We're now thinking about how to streamline this to make merging these kinds of mechanical changes easier.

Claude generally gets these PRs right on the first shot, and if it doesn't, we ask Claude to tune its routines so it's better the next day. Sometimes it takes a few days of tuning.

To try a similar workflow, ask Claude Code or Tag, or create some routines directly at [link]. A few of the actual prompts I used below.

Has anyone experimented with similar workflows?

Prompt debt is the new tech debt.

With every model update, you should be cutting at least 50% of your prompts.

Most AI products have bloated prompts that are making your product quality worse.

The typical flow is: model fails at something -> add 10 rules. Tool call fails -> add 10 examples. Output is weird -> add formatting constraints.

3 months later, your system prompt is a novel.

Meanwhile the model is way smarter and your micro-managey prompts are turning it into a dumb rules machine.
提示词债务是新的技术债务。

每次模型更新时，你应该至少削减 50% 的提示词。

大多数 AI 产品都有臃肿的提示词，反而让产品质量变差。

典型流程是：模型某处失败 → 加 10 条规则。工具调用失败 → 加 10 个示例。输出奇怪 → 添加格式约束。

三个月后，你的系统提示词变成了一本小说。

与此同时，模型变得更聪明了，而你那些微观管理的提示词把它变成了一个愚蠢的规则机器。

Grok Bot is a fantastic release.. the UX, the design, the onboarding :chefs-kiss:

But I go back and forth on this a lot: Will users (personal and business) want one super agent that holds all the context or sub agents that are designated to do a slice of tasks?

If we treat agents as humans, then the Grok Bot design makes a ton of sense. It allows us to compartmentalize tools, context, outcomes for that person's division. You reach out to that bot for that specific goal that you have in mind.

But, if you treat agents as Jarvis, you want a master agent that just gets the job done. It might create and coordinate all the bots (and dare I say the communication/context) that might be needed to do the task. But the interface is 1:1 vs. 1:many.

My guess is it'll veer towards the one master agent over time who can then act as an orchestrator who reads / understands the bots you create. But starts narrow as most of the companies are doing it now.

PS: I understand the core engineering constraints (context windows, tool use, simplicity, cost etc) why companies are choosing task/singular outcomes today. Primarily thinking this from a design / product side.
Grok Bot 是一个出色的发布……用户体验、设计、上手引导都堪称完美。

但我对此反复思考很多：用户（个人和企业）是想要一个拥有所有上下文的超级智能体，还是想要被指派特定任务的子智能体？

如果我们把智能体当作人来对待，那么 Grok Bot 的设计就非常合理。它允许我们为该人的部门划分工具、上下文和结果。你为了特定目标联系那个机器人。

但如果你把智能体当作 Jarvis，你会想要一个主智能体来完成工作。它可能会创建并协调所有可能需要的机器人（甚至可以说通信/上下文）。但界面是 1:1 而不是 1:多。

我的猜测是，随着时间推移，它会趋向于一个主智能体，它可以作为编排者，读取/理解你创建的机器人。但一开始会像大多数公司现在所做的那样狭窄。

附注：我理解核心工程约束（上下文窗口、工具使用、简单性、成本等）是公司今天选择任务/单一结果的原因。这主要是从设计/产品角度思考的。

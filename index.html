The biggest misread of anyone predicting the end of software engineering was imagining that the world only needed a certain amount of software, and bringing automation to the field would reduce the need for labor. Exactly the opposite is happening where essentially most things are actually turning into software. Because agents can use tools, work with computers, write software on the fly, and process data like humans (but far faster), we can now throw “software” at problems that never could reasonably be digitized before. What that leads to is the role of engineering taking on greater importance in the economy, because we can now engineer far more systems and processes than were possible before. If you're a bank trying to do new forms of trading, engineers are often your first bet. If you are in life sciences, you expect more and more research and lab work to be engineering centric. If you're in manufacturing, you're trying to automate more of the supply chain, all that is engineering. More and more work is shaped like software problems. Yes we're not going to be coding manually in the future. But almost everything will be code, which means those that understand it are in a fantastic position.
那些预言软件工程将终结的人，最大的误判在于：他们以为世界只需要一定数量的软件，而把自动化引入这个领域会减少对劳动力的需求。 事实恰恰相反——几乎所有东西实际上都在变成软件。因为智能体能够使用工具、操作电脑、即时编写软件，并像人一样（但快得多）处理数据，我们现在可以把「软件」这件武器用到那些以前根本无法合理数字化的难题上。 由此带来的结果是，工程岗位在经济中的重要性反而上升了，因为我们现在能够构建的系统与流程，远超以往所能。 如果你是银行、想做新型交易业务，工程师往往是你的第一选择。如果你身处生命科学领域，你会期待越来越多的研究与实验室工作以工程为核心。如果你做制造业，你会想自动化更多供应链环节——而这些都是工程。越来越多的工作，其本质都像软件问题。 是的，未来我们不会再手动写代码。但几乎所有东西都将是代码——这意味着那些真正理解代码的人，将处于极其有利的位置。

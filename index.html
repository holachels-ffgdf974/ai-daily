@box CEO · 你的业务存在于内容之中，用 AI 释放它

This is the diffusion challenge and opportunity in a nutshell. Most people will probably get stuck with the use-cases that initially wowed them with AI, and have little incentive to move onto the next thing that will unlock another level of productivity. Even a large number of organizations can get stuck by being "satisfied" with AI gains, well before having full blown agents that can do any amount of work in the background or agent swarms that can take on complex, ambiguous problems. Much of this comes down to the technical complexity of implementing the architectures around this. Despite lots of effort to simplify the UXes, there's still a lot of system work, data pipelines, MCPs, and security that needs to be worked through to get the most out of agents. But even when you're done with that, you still need to redesign your workflow or process overall to get the full benefits from agents. This is why there's so much opportunity to be an organization that figures this out early. It's also why there's going to be massive services and FDE opportunities right now as well, since most companies can't do this on their own.

这就是「扩散挑战与机遇」的核心所在。大多数人很可能会停留在最初让他们惊艳的 AI 用例上，缺乏动力去尝试下一个能释放更高生产力层级的东西。 甚至大量组织也会因为对 AI 带来的收益「感到满意」而停滞，远未达到拥有能在后台完成任意工作的完整智能体，或能处理复杂、模糊问题的智能体集群的阶段。 这在很大程度上归结于围绕这些能力实施架构的技术复杂性。尽管业界在简化用户体验上投入了大量努力，但仍有大量系统工作、数据管道、MCP 和安全问题需要解决，才能最大化智能体的价值。而即便这些做完，你还需要整体重新设计工作流或业务流程，才能真正获得智能体的全部收益。 这就是为什么尽早搞清楚这些事情的组织拥有巨大机会。这也是为什么当下会有大量服务与 FDE（前置部署工程师）机会——因为多数公司无法独自完成这些。

the race is not inevitable last year was fun. the models weren't that good yet, so i had to care about every detail. i pushed each thing until it had my thoughts and my designs. the tool helped. i did the driving. now the roles have flipped. we describe and watch things come back finished. we used to drive our dreams. more and more, we just watch. we set out to make things that give us more freedom. now we're racing toward something we might not be able to control. recursive self-improvement. intelligence building more intelligence, until we're no longer the ones who understand what's happening. that's the end game. the point where nobody is driving. the big labs are racing to get there first. the same system that cures a disease could design a new virus, or find every hole in the internet all at once. only the selected few with access could do anything about it. and somehow the possibility of losing everything we love becomes an argument for going faster. because someone else might get there first, and they might be less careful. because the company needs to survive. because too much money is already committed. everyone has a good reason to keep going toward an outcome nobody wants. so we call it inevitable. then nobody has to answer for it. but a race is made of decisions. someone approves the budget. someone decides the next model will help build the one after it. and we practice living inside the race long before we build it at the scale of civilization. sleep becomes recovery for work. friendship becomes a network. curiosity has to become a project before we let ourselves spend time on it. we get very rational. we start acting like the machines we're building. even our feelings have to defend themselves. why are you sad? is that useful? sometimes the follows the openings between the joints. but at a hard knot, he slows down. when it's done, he stands there holding the knife and looks around. the people building these systems are some of the best cooks alive. this is the hardest knot anyone has come to. it's where a master slows down. we cloned a sheep thirty years ago, and we still don't clone people. we can slow down. change direction. decide something isn't worth building, even if it would make someone very rich. a probability can help us think about a risk. it can't decide what we have the right to risk on behalf of everyone else. i want what we build to stay answerable to the people who have to live with it. most of them will never be in the room. there are no stands in this race. whatever happens, happens to us. i'd like the people in that room to come out and sit for a while. it's harder to gamble with ordinary life once you've spent an evening in the middle of it. the tea house is open all night.

The greatest tragedy right now in Silicon Valley that NO one wants to talk about is how some startups are "reselling" compute and calling it revenue.. not inference, just straight up selling compute to other companies. And, no their primary business is NOT selling compute. In this era, where everyone wants to show high "annualized run rate" growth as that's the story that gets marked up, companies are now being even more creative in how to lump "revenue" into a month. It started with GMV-like revenue with low margins, then inference at negative gross margins and now straight up reselling compute. All lumped into a tidy "ARR". Naive investors will lap it up since it allows them to pass deals through IC, and seed investors will celebrate their markups - but zero shot this ends well.

当下硅谷最大、却没人愿意谈的悲剧是：一些创业公司在「转售」算力，并把它叫做营收……不是推理，就是赤裸裸地把算力卖给别的公司。而且，不，它们的主营业务并不是卖算力。 在这个人人都想展示高「年化跑率」增长的时代——因为这是能让估值被抬高的叙事——公司现在在「如何把收入塞进某个月」上更有创意了。 一开始是低毛利的类 GMV 收入，然后是负毛利的推理，现在干脆是转售算力。全都打包成一个漂亮的「ARR」。 天真的投资人会照单全收，因为这让他们能把交易推过投委会；种子投资人会庆祝自己的账面增值——但这绝不可能有好结局。

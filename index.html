As these network attacks and impersonation attempts happen, who's securing the elderly? Especially thinking about my parents and grandparents. There are four layers I can think through where this could work. One is at the browser level, where somebody builds a secure browser that ensures that whatever websites they're going to are secured. Texts typically lead to a link, even for payments. This is why browser is key. Two could be at the network layer, where there is a physical device, similar to Pi-hole, or an Onavo-like VPN on your phone, that blocks websites that could be deemed unsafe. Three could be at the payments layer, where transactions that look like scams get flagged before the money leaves. And fourth and most crazy could be at the phone level, where everything is monitored, and you're only getting calls from your loved ones. Cloned voices would work with a provider like Reality Defender. There are individual solutions for this today but none neatly packaged. This could all be packaged with insurance that covers any activities on the OS or phone. In that way, incentives are aligned to keep this on the edge. The buyer would be the kids, someone like me, who get alerted when something looks off.
随着这些网络攻击和冒充行为不断发生，谁来保护老年人？ 尤其想到我的父母和祖父母。 我能想到四个层面可以切入。 第一是浏览器层面：有人打造一个安全浏览器，确保他们访问的任何网站都是安全的。短信通常会带一个链接，连支付也是如此。这就是浏览器为何关键。 第二是网络层面：类似 Pi-hole 的物理设备，或手机上一个类似 Onavo 的 VPN，拦截可能被判定为不安全的网站。 第三是支付层面：看起来像诈骗的交易在钱转出之前就被标记。 第四，也是最激进的，是手机层面：一切都被监控，你只会接到来自亲人的电话。克隆语音可以用 Reality Defender 之类的服务来识别。 今天这些都有各自的单独方案，但没有一个被整齐地打包在一起。 这一切都可以和保险打包，覆盖操作系统或手机上的任何活动。这样，激励就对齐了，让这套方案始终处于前沿。 买家会是子女，像我这样的人，一旦出现异常就能收到提醒。

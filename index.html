Every CEO｜让你保持在 AI 前沿的唯一订阅

I look at what I do with Fable 5.2 in the cracks of my week and think: this would've taken five people and $200,000. Then I look at what my team does with the same AI. Last week, between meetings, I analyzed our customer survey, made a game for our team, spotted an opportunity to sell @Every to teams, built a CRM, and started reaching out. Look at that and you might ask: Why would I need a team at all? The chart seems to agree. On OpenAI's GDPval benchmark, AI went from beating human experts 12.4% of the time in 2024 to around 83% in 2026. That's the aggregate view of the future of work: gather a lot of data and extrapolate a trend line. But the aggregate misses something. To see it, you have to get intimate: zoom in on one real situation, with all its messiness, and extrapolate from there. We gave everyone at Every access to the same customer survey and the same AI models. • I found an opportunity to sell to teams. • @poojary_yash saw that first-time subscribers were churning because we send too much email. • @dabrundage noticed that many of our readers feel lonely: they're excited about AI, but people around them think they're crazy. • @jackcheng turned the results into a Claymation game show. Could you ask Claude to produce what each of them found? Maybe. But generating possibilities is cheap. Knowing which ones matter is hard. @hammer_mt ran an experiment to predict what I'd say in Slack. When Claude got 400 attempts, it produced an answer like mine 93% of the time. But when it had to rank its own answers, mine landed in its top 10% only around 60% of the time. Each person on a team is responsible for a different part of the world. That responsibility narrows where they look, and their perspective helps them decide what matters enough to act on. A team explores more possibilities than a single default prompt, and it can take responsibility for what it finds. The obvious objection: models will get better at ranking. I think they will. But people aren't frozen while that happens. Comparing 2026 Yash with 2027 Claude misses that there will also be a 2027 Yash, with another year of experience, using 2027 Claude. As models improve and spread, they raise the floor for everyone. The default answer becomes the baseline. The value is in using your perspective and responsibility to push past it. That's why I think we need to change how we measure AI. Instead of only asking how well a model performs on problems everyone agrees are worth solving, ask: How good is it at doing my work, from my perspective? The aggregate can show us the capability curve. The intimate helps us see what we might build, and who we might become, as we ride it. I presented this at Thesis: 2027. The full talk is on the Every podcast:

看着我在一周的缝隙时间里用 Fable 5.2 做的事，我会想：这活儿以前得五个人、二十万美元才能干完。 然后我再看我的团队用同一个 AI 做的事。 上周，在会议间隙，我分析了我们的客户问卷，为团队做了一个游戏，发现了一个把 @Every 卖给团队客户的机会，搭了一个 CRM，并开始外联。 看到这些，你可能会问：那我为什么还需要一个团队？ 图表似乎也同意。在 OpenAI 的 GDPval 基准上，AI 击败人类专家的比例从 2024 年的 12.4% 上升到 2026 年的约 83%。这是关于未来工作的"总体视角"：收集大量数据，外推一条趋势线。 但总体视角漏掉了某些东西。要看到它，你得"贴近观察"：放大到一个真实的、充满混乱的具体情境，再从那里外推。 我们让 Every 的每个人都拿到了同一份客户问卷和同样的 AI 模型。 • 我发现了向团队客户销售的机会。 • @poojary_yash 发现首次订阅用户流失，是因为我们发的邮件太多。 • @dabrundage 注意到很多读者感到孤独：他们对 AI 兴奋，但身边的人觉得他们疯了。 • @jackcheng 把结果做成了一个黏土动画游戏节目。 你能让 Claude 产出他们各自发现的东西吗？也许可以。但生成可能性很廉价，知道哪些可能性重要才难。 @hammer_mt 做了一个实验，预测我在 Slack 里会说什么。Claude 有 400 次尝试时，93% 的情况下能产出和我类似的回答。但当它需要给自己的回答排序时，我的回答只有约 60% 的情况下能进入它的前 10%。 团队里每个人都对世界上不同的一部分负责。这种责任缩小了他们关注的视野，而他们的视角帮助他们判断什么重要到值得行动。团队比单一默认提示词能探索更多可能性，也能为所发现的东西承担责任。 显而易见的反驳：模型排序能力会变强。我认为会。但在那发生的同时，人也没有被冻住。拿 2026 年的 Yash 和 2027 年的 Claude 比较，忽略了届时还会有一个 2027 年的 Yash，带着多一年的经验，使用 2027 年的 Claude。 随着模型变强、普及，它们抬高了所有人的地板。默认答案成了基线。价值在于用你的视角与责任去突破它。 这就是为什么我认为我们需要改变衡量 AI 的方式。不要只问模型在大家都认同值得解决的问题上表现多好，而应该问：从我的视角看，它在做我的工作上有多好？ 总体视角能展示能力曲线。贴近视角则帮我们看见：在乘着这条曲线时，我们可能造出什么，又可能成为什么样的人。 我在 Thesis: 2027 上讲了这些。完整演讲在 Every 播客：

There are basically two kinds of companies to build right now. 1. Diffusion of AI This is the "vertical SaaS" of today. There are a bunch of different models for it (FDE company, rollup, horizontal product) but ultimately it all boils down to the same thing: get AI diffused very close to where the work is being done. Profound, Cognition, Harvey etc. A lot of great companies will get built here. 2. Frontier of AI This is where the really interesting action is. Use AI to accelerate scientific progress, then build products from that progress. It is a harder category to see. But we are seeing a lot more of it at @spc: - Externa is adding hundreds of new chemical building blocks to proteins, so you can engineer a drug to match the disease instead of settling for what nature gave you. - Epana Bio is using genomics and AI to design autoimmune therapies that go after the cellular origin of the disease. - VenomicsAI is doing AI-accelerated drug discovery from venoms. - Equivariant is doing multi-physics modeling for the semiconductor industry. (There are always picks-and-shovels for AI as well. Lots of amazing companies in that category.) Category 2 is not well understood or much talked about externally. It should be because I think that is where the vast majority of model driven returns will come from.

现在基本上有两类公司值得去建。 1. AI 的扩散 这就是今天的"垂直 SaaS"。它有多种模式（FDE 公司、并购整合、横向产品），但归根结底都是同一件事：让 AI 扩散到离实际工作最近的地方。 Profound、Cognition、Harvey 等等。这里会诞生很多伟大的公司。 2. AI 的前沿 真正有意思的事情在这里。用 AI 加速科学进展，再基于这些进展构建产品。 这一类更难看清。但在 @spc 我们看到了越来越多： - Externa 正在为蛋白质添加数百种新的化学构建模块，让你可以设计出与疾病匹配的药物，而不是将就于大自然给你的东西。 - Epana Bio 正在用基因组学和 AI 设计自身免疫疗法，直击疾病的细胞起源。 - VenomicsAI 正在做 AI 加速的蛇毒药物发现。 - Equivariant 正在为半导体行业做多物理场建模。 （AI 领域的"卖铲子"生意当然也一直存在。这个类别里有很多了不起的公司。） 第二类在外部没有被很好地理解，也很少被谈论。但它应该被重视，因为我认为绝大多数由模型驱动的回报都会来自这里。
